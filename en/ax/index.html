AX notes

AX notes record the rules SugarMount settled on, and the numbers it actually measured, while building and shipping Android apps with an AI coding agent.

This is AX (AI transformation) applied to one-person app development. In the 20 days from the first commit on September 20, 2026, four Android apps (Compress Image, Flip & Rotate Image, Convert Image and HWP Viewer SugarDoc) went live on Google Play.

Every number comes from the git history, changelog and on-device QA reports of SugarMount's app repository. What we could not verify, we say so.

    How these notes are written

    • Only our own apps. Every number, screen and script comes from SugarMount apps.
    • Numbers carry a source and a date. Numbers change over time, so each one says when it was measured.
    • Failures are included. A defect found right after release, and its cause, is often more useful than a rule.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is AX?

    AX stands for AI transformation: changing how work is done so that AI takes part in it. Here it means a way of developing where an AI coding agent writes the code, tests and release scripts, and a person makes the decisions and does the verification.

    Which AI tool do you use?

    Anthropic's Claude Code. Rules reach the agent through instruction files at the repository root (AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md), and large tasks are split across several agents.

    Do you ship AI-written code as is?

    No. Before every upload a build goes through a release gate, a launch check of the minified release build, and on-device end-to-end tests. See the pre-release checks note.

    Where do the numbers come from?

    From the git history, CHANGELOG and QA reports of SugarMount's app repository (sugar-utils, private). Each note gives the date its numbers were taken.
