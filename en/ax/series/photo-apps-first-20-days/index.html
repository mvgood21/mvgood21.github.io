Three photo apps, the first 20 days: what was fast and what we missed

A solo developer and Claude Code put four Android apps on Google Play within 20 days of the first commit, co-writing 52 of 59 commits by October 10, 2026. Four parts on what that speed hid.

4 parts

What this series found

  • From the first commit on September 20, 2026 to October 10, 52 of 59 commits (88%) were co-written with an AI coding agent. The speed came from stamping every app from one template and scripting pre-release checks and Play Console work; running real-device tests late cost us 9 defects found after submission. (Part 1: four apps in 20 days)
  • Four minutes after 243 unit tests passed with no failures, Compress Image crashed on the emulator. GMA Next-Gen delivers interstitial callbacks on a background thread, and a screen change moved inside that callback threw an exception. (Part 2: ad callbacks off the main thread)
  • A 107 KB JPEG run with a 500 KB limit came out as a 150,250-byte file (October 4, Galaxy S20). It was under the limit, so the search counted it a success; now, if the result is larger than the original, the app saves the original with only its metadata stripped. (Part 3: fitting a JPEG to a size)
  • We collected Google Play search results only after all three photo apps were built. The 32 compression apps released in 2026 had a median of 1,166 installs and 26 of them had under 10,000, so we put 11 planned apps on hold. (Part 4: a late market check)

This series is for solo developers planning several small Android apps with an AI coding agent such as Claude Code. If you have not started building, read the 15-minute market check in Part 4 first. Then read Part 1 for the whole 20 days, Part 2 if you are adding an ad SDK and Part 3 if you are building image compression. Parts 2 and 3 are both cases where code that passed every test went wrong on a device, which explains the regret in Part 1 about testing on real phones too late.
