10,001 lines of greeting cards: built with an AI agent, removed with one

In 1.6.020, which shipped the greeting card removal together with R8 optimization, the APK went from 25.66 MB to 13.15 MB; about 11 MB of that drop was greeting card assets.

By SugarMount · Retrospectives and numbers · 10 min read · Last updated November 6, 2026

A single commit, f6ef50e, deleted 143 files and 10,001 lines and ended the greeting card feature in SugarSpeller, our Korean spell-check app. It landed on the night of September 20, 2026, 35 minutes after the operator said the feature had been forced into the app in the first place. The feature itself had arrived in 41 commits between August 31 and December 10, 2025, and 38 of those commits credit Claude. In January 2026 the code was taken into a sibling app, SugarCard, yet it stayed in the original app for eight more months.

Sources: git history of the spell-check app and of SugarCard, the spell-check app's CHANGELOG entry for 1.6.020, a post-release monitoring document (GA4 counts), and the work session log of September 20 to 22, 2026. Numbers were recounted from git on November 4, 2026.

13.70 MB of deleted files, 0.22 MB of app Kotlin

f6ef50e deleted 124 files, modified 18 and added one. The deletions covered three greeting screens (ActivityMainGreeting, a card editor and a card fragment), a Room database for cards, an MP4 encoder and audio muxer for a falling-snow video, the card images, seven carols, a handwriting font called Gamja Flower, 13 unit tests and one instrumented test. Of the 10,001 deleted lines, 7,857 were Kotlin and 2,124 were XML.

Summing the original blob sizes of the deleted files gives 13.70 MB, and the 15 app Kotlin sources account for 0.22 MB of it. The 69 files in the card image folder came to 6.68 MB, one background image, retro.png, to 3.91 MB, the font to 1.07 MB, the seven carols to 1.02 MB and the Lottie snow animation to 0.34 MB. (The commit message rounds the first three to 6.5, 3.8 and 1.1 MB.)

The sizes below come from the CHANGELOG. The baseline is the pre-change HEAD (1.6.019) rebuilt in the same environment.

Crediting that whole drop to the greeting cards would be wrong. The same release carried a597223, which deleted the single -dontoptimize line that had kept R8 from optimizing. The CHANGELOG splits the result: about 11 MB was greeting card assets, and the optimization's share sits inside the 1.5 MB DEX reduction. The optimization side has its own note, Play Console said our DEX was 39% optimized.

Commits claimed 95% coverage; the repo had no coverage tool

The first commit, 0a0f404 on August 31, 2025, added 2,495 lines across 21 files, built the card list and a Room module, and claimed "95% coverage (30+ test cases)". Two more commits that day fixed a clash between Material 3 and the app's existing theme. On September 3, be41ccb, titled as a complete Y2K glitch-neon card editor, touched 60 files and reported "zero memory leaks and crashes". On September 10 greeting cards became a third screen mode, UIMode.GREETING, next to the chat and classic layouts. Holiday categories followed in late November, and on December 7 and 8 a ten-second falling-snow MP4 share and seven carols.

24 of the 41 commits landed in September 2025. Together they added 18,087 lines and deleted 9,188 (docs included, over 102 days).

Five of those commits (six, counting the docs commit db2ee27 from the same week) claim 95% or 99% test coverage. Neither git log --all -S jacoco nor -S kover returns a single line anywhere in the repository's history, so no coverage tool was ever installed. The habit of writing "complete" into commit subjects is counted in A year of AI coding in 701 commits. The Room database that the first commit called "a complete greeting module" had zero callers in the app code (main) outside its own four files when it was deleted. And on September 11, c05d669 made the greeting screen the default for users with no saved mode and forced that mode in debug builds. About 24 hours later 0c5ceab put the default back to chat. The next release tag was November 28, so no shipped version had the greeting default.

A third main screen, and a copy kept for eight months

Greeting cards came in as one of three parallel main screens, not as a separate module. Card artwork and carols therefore shipped in the APK of every user who only wanted spell checks, and any fix to the main screen had to be made three times.

The structure stayed after the sibling app existed. SugarCard's first commit, c623dd8 on January 15, 2026, started with 297 files and 17,030 lines, and its message says "Greeting card functionality migrated from sugarspeller". Nobody removed the feature from the original app at that point. Between then and September 13, five commits in the spell-check app edited ActivityMainGreeting, and all five were shared fixes for ads or the back button made alongside the chat screen. The September 13 fix for "press back again to exit" (289ed55) changed 26, 23 and 22 lines in the three screens. We found no record of why the removal waited.

When a feature moves to another app, delete it from the original app in the same release. During those eight months this app got five shared fixes that also had to patch a screen that already lived in SugarCard.

Two menu entries became links; saved values fall back to chat

On September 20 the operator asked to take greeting cards out of this app entirely, keep the two buttons in the side drawer and the top mode menu but send them to SugarCard, and focus the app on Korean spelling. The operator also expected the app to get much smaller. Six minutes later the operator added Play's code optimization requirement (deadline February 2027) to the same job, which became a597223.

The kept buttons now call a new SugarCardLauncher. If SugarCard is installed it opens the app; otherwise it tries market://, and if that fails, the https store page. Since Android 11 (API 30) an app has to declare another package under <queries> in its manifest to see whether it is installed, so the manifest gained one entry. Each tap logs sugarcard_open(installed) to GA4.

Users who had picked greeting mode before updating still have the string "greeting" in their settings. The code that reads the mode already sent unknown values to chat:

fun fromString(value: String?): UIMode {
    return values().find { it.value == value } ?: CHAT
}

So there is no migration code. UIModeTest gained one test asserting that "greeting" resolves to CHAT, with a comment saying these users must not get stuck behind the splash screen.

The agent guessed 10 MB and 9 tests; a person checked the device

The agent's plan named the screens and modules to remove, but its numbers were low. It added card images (6.5 MB), retro.png (3.8 MB) and the snow animation (0.3 MB) to get "roughly 10 MB", and counted 9 unit tests to delete. The real commit also removed the font and the seven carols, and deleted 13 unit tests.

The agent had no device attached, said so, and left a release APK signed with the debug key. It asked the operator to try checks against Naver and Daum (two Korean portals whose spell-check results the app parses), history, subscriptions, KakaoTalk sharing and the greeting card menu. The operator replied the next day that everything worked, and 1.6.020 shipped on September 22.

One wrong assumption stayed in the launcher. If opening market:// threw, it fell back to the browser, and the comment on that branch read "device without the Play Store app". On October 9 the same pattern was added for a games menu (efc0acf). On a Galaxy S21 the link showed a chooser instead of an exception, because three store apps accepted it. 8bbe179, 59 minutes after the games commit, made both the game and SugarCard links target the Play package first. The full account is in A market:// link opens a store chooser on SKT-sold Samsung phones.

The menus stayed, opened the app first, and shipped in one release

Deleting the menus too was ruled out by the operator's first request. The Korean label lost its "switch to" wording, since tapping it no longer switches modes.

Sending every tap straight to the store was an option the agent raised in its plan. The operator kept the app-first order, which is why the manifest needs <queries>. As the launcher comment says, without that declaration the installed check always fails and every tap goes to the store.

Shipping the removal and the R8 change in separate releases would have made the size change easy to attribute. The operator chose to ship them together, which is why the size section uses a separately built baseline.

Stored user data would change the order

This removal needed no data migration only because no screen called the Room database by the time of removal (none had since September 3, 2025, and no release shipped while one did). If users had saved cards in it, an export path or a handoff to the sibling app would have to come first.

If the mode parser had thrown on unknown values, one test would not have been enough. Updated users could get stuck at launch, and a migration that rewrites the saved value would have to ship first.

We found no record of how often the greeting screens were used; the only numbers are menu taps after the removal. For a feature many people use, check screen usage before deleting it.

Steps for handing a feature to a sibling app

  1. Count the files and bytes to be deleted with git. Get the deleted blobs from git diff --raw and sum them with git cat-file -s, so you know whether lines or bytes dominate.
  2. Count the callers of the module with git grep -l. Zero callers outside the module means no data migration.
  3. Read where a saved setting goes when its value no longer exists, and pin that path with one unit test.
  4. If the menu stays, add a sibling-app launcher. Declare the package under <queries>, and open the store in this order: market:// targeted at the Play package, plain market://, then the https page.
  5. Compare sizes against the pre-change HEAD built in the same environment. If the release has other changes, split the shares.
  6. Install the release APK on a real device and tap the kept menu with the sibling app installed and not installed.
  7. Log menu taps with the installed state to see demand.

We don't yet know whether the 54 taps became installs

In the two weeks after release (September 22 to October 5), sugarcard_open fired 54 times from about 39 users, and SugarCard was installed at the moment of the tap in none of them, so every tap went to the store (GA4, per the monitoring document). Another 61 SugarCard taps came in from October 9 to 22; installed only records the state at tap time. Starting with 1.6.027 the event is called sibling_app_open (target=sugarcard); the next step is to line it up against SugarCard's install sources.

FAQ

What happens to users who saved a mode that the update removes?

It depends on where the parser sends unknown values. This app's fromString already mapped unknown values to chat, so a leftover "greeting" opens the chat screen, and a unit test pins that path.

I removed a feature and the APK barely shrank. What should I check?

Check the bytes of the deleted files first. Here 10,001 lines went away but the app's Kotlin sources were 0.22 MB; the size came from images, a font and audio.

Why does my check for another installed app always say "not installed"?

Since Android 11 (API 30), getLaunchIntentForPackage only finds the other app if its package is declared under <queries> in the manifest. f6ef50e declared just SugarCard's package; more were added later for the games menu.
