Quiz reach rose from 2.6% to 17.7%, and 63% of quiz opens came from one morning notification

More in-app entry points rarely bring many more people to a feature. Our spelling app's quiz reach went from 2.6% to 17.7%, and 63% of quiz opens came from one 8 a.m. notification.

By SugarMount · Android development · 11 min read · Last updated November 13, 2026

Reworking a long-running spelling app 8/8

To get more people into a feature buried in an app, the usual move is to add entry points around the screens. When we revived the quiz in SugarSpeller, our Korean spelling app, the plan we wrote with the coding agent named a bubble under spell-check results as the biggest lever. Splitting quiz opens by path in GA4 in October 2026, the bubble accounted for 13.5% and a morning notification we had left on the "later" list for 63%. Below: how we measured reach with one event, the numbers per path, why the notification won and where that stops holding, and the order to measure your own paths.

Measured on one event, reach went from 2.6% to 17.7%

The metric is users who logged quiz_open divided by active users of that version. The baseline filters appVersion=1.6.020 over September 22 to October 5 and was fixed on October 6. The comparison combines 1.6.024 and 1.6.025 over September 26 to October 9 and was read on October 10. The versions differ, and the two windows share only ten days.

There is a third number, 3.4%, and it measures something else. It comes from a September 21 query: 339 of 9,859 active users in the previous 90 days viewed the quiz screen (ActivityMistake). quiz_open didn't exist yet, so that figure counts screen views, and it can't sit next to 2.6% or 17.7%. The plan borrowed it as a provisional baseline until the real one arrived on October 6.

Five main commits and three complaints in five days

Until September 21 the quiz was the eighth of 13 items in the navigation drawer, and nothing in the spell-check flow led to it. People who found it stayed 35 seconds per view, so we read the problem as discovery and reward. Free users who answered correctly saw a subscription prompt in place of the explanation, and 47 of the 124 explanations were still placeholders.

  • Sep 21: added quiz_open and related events, opened explanations to everyone, added a daily set of five questions and moved the item to second place in the drawer. It shipped in 1.6.020 on September 22.
  • Sep 22: redesigned the screen and added a "Next question" button.
  • Sep 23: added a question bubble after spell-check results in Chat mode and a banner in Classic mode, whose result is a single text field.
  • Sep 24: fixed seven bugs and five UX problems.
  • Sep 25: added numbered progress circles, permanent records of solved questions and an 8 a.m. local notification.

The operator pushed back three times: on September 21 that the screen looked dated, on the 24th that it still looked off and seemed buggy, and on the 25th that not much seemed to have changed and that a morning notification ought to exist. Unit tests and the release build had passed through the September 22 redesign. After the second complaint, a fresh pass over code and screenshots found that "More questions" handed over all 119 remaining questions as one set, which collapsed the progress bar into 119 zero-width segments.

A September 21 query, before 1.6.020 shipped, put screen-view reach for September 2026 at 2.8%. With the item moved up and explanations open to everyone, the quiz_open baseline on 1.6.020 was 2.6%.

Bubble and notification reached crowds of the same size; their conversion differed 2.8-fold

The two paths were shown to nearly identical numbers of people on 1.6.024 and 1.6.025: 1,187 users saw the bubble (quiz_bubble_show) and 1,189 saw the notification (quiz_reminder_show), each about 54% the size of the 2,190 active users. Notification views can include people who didn't open the app, so they aren't necessarily a subset of active users. From the bubble, 96 users (8.1%) continued to the quiz screen. From the notification, 270 (22.7%) did. The verdict table has no bubble view count, so there's no event-based rate to compare. The 2.8 times compares only users who entered at least once in 14 days. Over that window the notification went out about 6.4 times per user who saw it.

Users of the three other paths sum to 194, duplicates included. Taking the 270 notification users from the 388 who opened the quiz leaves 118, so 118 to 194 distinct users came by other paths, or 5.4% to 8.9% of active users, short of the 10% target. Taking that upper bound of 194 from 388 means at least 194 users came only through the notification, half of reach or more. The 5.4% to 8.9% is not what reach would have been with no notification, because someone reminded by it may have come back through the drawer or the bubble. entry=drawer opens rose from 5.6 to 9.4 per 100 active users. The verdict table has no per-path figure for 1.6.020, but every entry in that version's code sends drawer, so we used all 46. The value combines the drawer item and the quiz icon on the Classic main screen. The icon exists in all three versions, and the drawer layout is the same in 1.6.020, 1.6.024 and 1.6.025.

The explanation that follows comes from the app's structure; we did not measure it. The drawer works only if someone opens it. The bubble appears once per session, after a spell check, for people already in the app, and they can answer right there, which reduces the reason to change screens. The notification arrives at 8 a.m. whether or not the app was opened, and its body carries the day's first question with both choices.

This does not make the bubble useless. The two rates also differ in depth: the 8.1% answered a question and then tapped More questions, while the 22.7% tapped once. An answer given in the bubble logs quiz_answer with mode=chat and no quiz_open, so reach never counts it. The rule we wrote before release says to read that pattern as people answering in the bubble and stopping there, but the verdict row has no count for them.

The plan left the notification for later

The September 21 plan, written with the agent, opened its bubble section with "the biggest lever" and the fact that 78% of users pass through Chat. The 78.0% was correct. The mistake was choosing the lever from in-app traffic alone. Notifications appeared as one line in the list of candidates to consider after the verdict, limited to users who had already solved a question.

The operator pulled the notification forward and also reversed its default. In the September 25 commit the agent built it off by default, with a switch on the result card, which you only see at the end of a set. The same day, the next commit turned it on by default. No production build had the default off, so we have no reach number for that setting. Android 13 and later get one permission prompt, and declining leaves the result card switch off.

We don't leave notification defaults or frequency to the agent. They land in a user's notification tray, and the retention data that would judge the choice doesn't exist yet, so a person decides and records why.

A local alarm instead of server push

The code says entry=push, but nothing is pushed from a server. QuizReminder schedules a local notification with AlarmManager. The daily set is chosen on the device from a date seed, so the notification can include the question itself. We rejected an FCM console campaign for three reasons: it couldn't carry the question, sending daily would need a recurring console campaign or a scheduler, and without segments it would reach everyone and likely raise opt-outs, a design judgment we never measured. Waking the device with an FCM data message and building the body there never came up in the plan. We didn't measure on-time delivery, and the local notification uses setInexactRepeating, which can deliver minutes late.

Two other ideas went too. A time picker was left out because we expected fewer people to turn the feature on. The bubble was set to once per session from the start, because showing it after every check would look like an ad.

The 58.5% completion rate includes bubble answers

The screen hypothesis was that the missing "Next question" button held completion down, since moving on required knowing you could swipe. The table recorded completion rising from 24% to 58.5% and answers per open from 2.8 to 7.9, and marked the hypothesis as supported.

Those two numbers overstate the screen's effect. quiz_done and quiz_answer include answers from the Chat bubble, the 7.9 in the table isn't filtered by mode, and since the September 24 fixes a final answer in the bubble also sends quiz_done, so the numerator can grow while quiz_open users stay flat. The redesign, the bubble, the notification and a review of the question bank (47 explanations written, 68 corrected, 26 questions changed) all fall inside the 1.6.024 window, so GA4 can't separate them. The plan said as much for the bubble and the screen, and assigned reach to one and completion to the other.

The path breakdown above speaks to reach only. Active users who finished a set went from 0.6% to 10.4% because reach and completion rose together, and we didn't measure how much the redesign contributed.

21 baseline users and 1,442 who couldn't get the notification

The baseline has 21 users and five completions, so a handful of people would swing the 24%. This is also a before-and-after comparison across versions, not a randomized experiment. A provisional analysis on October 4 (September 28 to October 4, 1.6.025) pointed the same way, with 22.0% reach and 8.2% excluding the notification, but it covers a different window and sample.

Apps where many users block notifications may not get this result. On October 22, more users with the alarm set could not receive it (quiz_reminder_blocked, 1,442) than could (1,261). How those background events inflated GA4 totalUsers is covered in Daily users rose after the redesign while organic D1 retention fell from 15.2% to 11.2%, so once an app has background alarms, use activeUsers as the denominator.

It also won't fit a feature with nothing new to say each day. Our notification body is that day's question.

We still don't know what the notification costs. Version 1.6.030 tested four notifications a day on October 17 (qr_extra), and the test ended with no verdict. A 50:50 split showed up as 2,188 to 1,061 active users because the property recorded who was actually receiving extras, so people caught by the fatigue guard (four unopened in a row means mornings only for seven days) and dormant users who never opened the app were tagged as control. Raw uninstall rates of 7.8% for control and 18.9% for the extra arm can't be read as cause and effect.

How to measure this in your own app

If a feature's reach is in single digits, add an entry parameter before you redesign it again, and put the paths that start outside the app in the same verdict.

  1. Add entry to the open event and register the GA4 custom definition before release. Registration doesn't apply to past data.
  2. Put a parameter on the completion event for the screen where the set ended. Our quiz_done has none, so completion can't be split by screen.
  3. Take a baseline on the last version before new entry points, filtered by appVersion. If the screen and the entry points ship together, assign each its own metric first.
  4. Write the target and what a miss would mean before release.
  5. Put paths outside the app in the same verdict row as paths inside it. Ours lists entry=push beside the drawer, the bubble and the banner.
  6. Pull exposed users, entering users and event counts for each path, using the five formulas below.
  7. Label the sum of per-path users as an upper bound, because one person can use two paths. Our three paths other than the notification summed to 194, and adding the 270 notification users overshoots actual reach of 388 by 76.
  8. In a notification A/B test, store the assigned arm as a user property from the first day. We only added qr_arm in 1.6.033.
reach             = quiz_open users / activeUsers of the same version
notification rate = quiz_open(entry=push) users / quiz_reminder_show users
bubble rate       = quiz_open(entry=chat) users / quiz_bubble_show users
completion        = quiz_done users / quiz_open users
answers per open  = quiz_answer(mode!=chat) events / quiz_open events

Completion's numerator can include users who finished in the bubble without a quiz_open, so it isn't guaranteed to be a subset of the denominator.

Still open: the November 15 verdict and bubble-only answers

A new reading with qr_arm, on three notifications a day with the afternoon slot removed, is due on November 15, 2026. In the earlier test, opens by slot were 10.4% in the morning, 18.9% at lunch, 7.7% in the afternoon and 12.2% in the evening. If uninstalls in the extra arm are significantly more than 2 percentage points worse than control, the proposal will be to turn extras off.

The monitoring table has no reach verdict after October 10, so we don't know whether 17.7% held. Counting quiz_answer users with mode=chat would show what the bubble really contributes. We also haven't measured whether people who arrive by notification finish a set, and the verdict table has no figure for how many users keep it on. The table lists the next improvement: reviewing the questions with correct-answer rates of 13% to 32%.

Frequently asked questions

Should you fix the screen or the entry points first?

Measure first. We then fixed the screen and added entry points after it, reasoning that the destination had to work before we sent people there. Both shipped in one release, and we never tested the opposite order.

Should notification conversion be counted by users or by events?

Look at both, and judge reach by users. This notification converted 22.7% of users and 8.8% of events, because the same person receives it on many days.
