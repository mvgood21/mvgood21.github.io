Four Google Ads app campaigns, $36.4, 105 installs, about 3% ROAS: CPI ran 30 times break-even

Block Turn KR, the best of four first-round campaigns, paid $0.59 per install, about 30 times its break-even CPI, the ad revenue one installer brings in before leaving.

By SugarMount · Play Store and monetization · 12 min read · Last updated November 8, 2026

Ads and metrics for two puzzle games 4/4

Four Google Ads app campaigns for two puzzle games spent $36.4 between launch and the morning of October 17, 2026, and GA4 attributed 105 installs to paid traffic (medium cpc). ROAS (ad revenue over ad spend) ran from 0.1% to about 3% per campaign. The go/no-go criteria set on October 15 had a CPI ceiling, D1 and same-day uninstalls, and no break-even line. A break-even CPI had been sitting in a September 30 document all along; we took it out again only after doubling the budget.

Sources: the game repository's ad analysis and verdict documents and its git history, the promotion results and ad restart planning documents, and daily snapshots from a personal dashboard (Oct 24 to Nov 2), the operating harness's budget rule, and the daily analyses run on Nov 4 and Nov 6 (reporting Nov 3 and Nov 5). Dollar figures are Google Ads cost; won figures use the analysis documents' assumed rate of 1,400 won per dollar. Checked on 2026-11-06. Ad account and campaign identifiers are left out, and so are the amounts of break-even CPI and per-player ad revenue.

First round: four campaigns, $36.4, 105 installs

The first round ran one Korean (KR) and one English campaign for each of Arrow Knots and Block Turn; the English ones targeted Southeast Asia (SEA) or the whole world. On the morning of October 17 the agent joined GA4 paid installs to Google Ads cost per campaign. For Block Turn, GA4 logged about 35% less ad revenue than AdMob, so that revenue was corrected to AdMob's figures; the two Arrow Knots rows were not corrected.

By CPI the cheapest campaign was Arrow Knots SEA EN at $0.20; Block Turn KR cost three times as much. By ROAS the order flipped: Block Turn KR led at about 3% and Arrow Knots SEA EN came last at 0.1%.

Actual CPI about 30 times break-even

Break-even CPI is the ad revenue one installer brings in before uninstalling: average revenue per daily active user (ARPDAU) times expected active days. The October 17 analysis computed it from the retention of a 22-player Block Turn KR cohort and Korean ARPDAU, and the actual CPI of $0.59 came out about 30 times higher.

The same document also tried D1 doubled and ARPDAU tripled, and still found a 7x loss. Its conclusion: at any budget, this funnel could not pay back its ad spend. Nor was there evidence that ads bought store ranking (the analysis counted one organic Block Turn install).

Expected active days were short because of the first session. When the analysis rebuilt the event sequences of 17 Block Turn KR paid players (16 device models), 7 of them (41%) finished the three tutorial boards and then left on board R004 without solving it once. It notes that they left before seeing a banner. How the stuck boards were found is in Puzzle game day-one churn: per-device event timelines, exact board math and four walls in chapter one.

Against the industry benchmarks gathered in the same analysis, our CPI was cheap and D1 sat near the median. Day-0 uninstalls (58%) ran about twice the benchmark, and ARPDAU was at the bottom of the range.

Ads counted 94 installs; GA4 saw 24 first opens

The cost per install shown in the Google Ads console was far lower than the CPI above. In the October 17 promotion results document, Ads put it at 101 won for Block Turn EN and 371 won for Block Turn KR. Divided by GA4 installs, the same campaigns cost $0.27 (about 380 won) and $0.59 (about 830 won).

The gap is in the denominator. Against 94 install conversions Ads counted for Block Turn EN, GA4 logged 24 first opens in Indonesia (26%); for Block Turn KR it was 19 against 38 (50%). (Ads counted Sep 17 to Oct 16, GA4 from launch to Oct 17.) Three out of four Southeast Asian ad installs never showed up as a GA4 first open. In the October 17 to 23 retest the console had 55 conversions against 25 GA4 paid installs (45%), and for October 26 to November 1 it had 102 conversions against 50 GA4 installs on the dashboard.

We did not find out why Ads conversions exceed GA4 first opens. This record only gives the ratio, so the payback math uses GA4 first opens as the denominator. The device filter arrived midway: test phones were removed from October 18 and Play pre-launch devices from October 25, so the first-round table predates both. The filter is described in GA4 said 19 installs yesterday and 8 were real.

The October 15 criteria had no break-even line

The agent's October 15 analysis blamed the quality of Southeast Asian traffic. Indonesian players all had their phones set to Indonesian, the app only shipped Korean and English, and they played 84 to 92 seconds each. That diagnosis held up. The same document called Block Turn's Korean paid traffic "the only paid traffic that earns anything", and the operator stopped the SEA campaigns and raised Block Turn KR from 2,000 to 4,000 won a day.

The document's "decision (user)" section set the criteria for the October 22 verdict: keep or raise the budget if CPI was at most 1,500 won, D1 at least 25% and same-day uninstalls under 40%. A unit-economics document written on September 30 in the same repository had already computed a break-even CPI from assumed values, and the 1,500 won ceiling sat far above it. The retest's 1,107 won per install (27,681 won over 25 GA4 installs) clears that table's CPI line easily. Its D1 of 20% missed 25%, so the table would not have said keep or raise; it would have said extend a week.

The October 17 analysis redid that calculation with measured retention and measured ARPDAU instead of assumptions, and break-even came out lower still. Compute break-even CPI before switching a campaign on, and put it in the first row of the go/no-go table. Don't raise a budget on a table that only has a CPI ceiling.

A 7-day learning budget instead of a stop

The October 17 analysis recommended stopping Arrow Knots KR and pausing Block Turn KR until a fixed build shipped, on the grounds that more money into a leaking funnel would only confirm what we knew. That day the operator paused every campaign for both games, said the money should now go to learning who the players are, and turned Block Turn KR alone back on at 4,000 won a day with an end date of October 23.

The verdict used the three first-experience criteria the October 17 analysis had set (at least 80% solving the fourth board, at least 30% D1, under 40% same-day uninstalls), and that table had no CPI or break-even row either. On October 24 two of the three missed, and version 1.1.6, which moved the stuck fourth board R294 to position 41, shipped the same day. The board-level numbers are in that note's October 24 addendum.

Second round: Block Turn KR alone, 7-day ROAS 8 to 10%

On October 24 the operator switched Block Turn KR back on at 4,000 won a day until November 1, with an early-stop check on October 27 and a final verdict on November 2. The personal dashboard's daily snapshots use a rolling 7-day window, so values up to October 30 still include retest days. The October 31 and November 1 snapshots, which cover only second-round days, show 7-day ROAS of 8% and 10%.

On version 1.1.8 (October 25 to 30), 39 of 46 players solved the first board R001 (85%), which the daily analysis judged level with 6 of 8 on 1.1.6, one player apart. The next wall was the fifth board, R085, solved by 12 of 23 on 1.1.8. The October 26 to 27 new-player cohort had a D1 of 0 out of 16. The October 30 daily analysis noted 7-day ROAS of 8% and kept the budget until the November 2 verdict, and that day version 1.1.9, which moves R085 to position 35, was submitted.

November 2 verdict: under the cut line, back on at 3,000 won

The November 2 verdict used the cut line in the budget rule, which shrinks the budget when 7-day ROAS is under 30%; the first-experience criteria were not used. 7-day ROAS was 10%, and the campaign had already stopped at its end date. The Ads screen showed 27,705 won and 102 conversions for October 26 to November 1. The agent recommended not resuming; with the operator's approval it went back on at 14:25 the same day, at 3,000 won a day with no end date.

The first 1.1.9 cohort (October 30 to November 1) had fewer than 20 players, so it was read as fractions: R001 12 of 13, the fifth board R005 6 of 7, R364 8 of 10, judged the same as 1.1.8. By then the budget rule had a cut line and a raise condition (7-day ROAS of at least 100% with at least 20 installs in 7 days), but the verdict table had no break-even row at 100%. Both entered the budget rule on October 30.

Why SEA, event bidding and a bigger budget were dropped

Keeping the SEA English campaigns looked reasonable on one number. In the October 18 document, a player who finished the tutorial cost about 225 won from SEA and about 404 won from Korea. The same document put D1 at 4% for SEA against 22% for Korea; the October 17 comparison had already shown three in four SEA ad installs missing from GA4 first opens, and there was no Indonesian translation, so the option was dropped.

Switching the campaign goal from installs to tutorial completions was also considered. The bar written in that document was at least 10 conversions a day, and Block Turn KR produced about 3 tutorial completions a day. The goal stayed on installs, with tutorial completion and reaching level 10 added only as secondary conversions. Raising the budget was ruled out by the October 17 calculation: even with D1 doubled and ARPDAU tripled, the loss was 7x.

The first-round 3% and the second-round 8% are different formulas

First-round ROAS divides the ad revenue of everyone installed since launch by spend over the same period, with only Block Turn's revenue corrected to AdMob. The dashboard's 7-day ROAS uses GA4 ad revenue: the last 7 days of revenue from all players the campaign ever brought in, over the last 7 days of spend. GA4 logged about 35% less ad revenue than AdMob, so reading the two as "up from 3% to 8%" would be wrong.

The samples are small. Sixteen players started the fourth board in the October 24 verdict, so one player moved the rate by 6 points. The exchange rate is an assumption. Break-even here counts ad revenue only; an app that earns mostly from in-app purchases, or a genre that keeps players for weeks, puts different numbers into the same formula.

Five things to compute and compare before the next campaign

  1. Compute break-even CPI from your own measurements. Expected ad revenue per installer = expected active days x ARPDAU, and that value is the break-even CPI. The September 30 document defines expected active days as the sum of the retention curve. Here, count install day as 1 and add 1 + D1 + D2 + D3 + … A made-up example, unrelated to this game: ARPDAU of 10 won with D1 40%, D2 20%, D3 10% and nothing after gives 1.7 days and a break-even CPI of 17 won.
  2. Put break-even CPI or 100% ROAS in the first row of the go/no-go table, then the funnel criteria (first-board solve rate, D1, same-day uninstalls), the verdict date and where the verdict will be written.
  3. Per campaign, set the conversions (installs) column on the Ads campaign screen next to GA4 first_open split by firstUserMedium=cpc and country, and take the ratio.
  4. Remove test phones and Play pre-launch devices before counting. The first figure in the October 25 midpoint check, 6 of 12 solving R001, included four pre-launch robots; without them it was 6 of 8.
  5. Run a fixed budget for 7 days and change one thing between runs. Between the retest and the second round only the fourth board changed (R294 to R364), but during the second round 1.1.8 went live and 1.1.9 was submitted, so this rule was broken.

Cut on November 6: 7-day ROAS 6%, down to 2,000 won a day

Block Turn KR, back on at 3,000 won a day, was first set for a verdict on November 4. The November 4 daily analysis corrected that: the date had counted the start day as day one, and with the last budget change on November 2 the rule that a campaign's budget changes only once every three days lands on November 5. That analysis put 7-day ROAS at 9%, under the cut line.

Scheduled game jobs then started failing on the evening of November 4, the November 5 daily analysis never ran, and the November 5 verdict was applied in the November 6 rerun. 7-day ROAS was 6%, 7-day CPI $0.47, 7-day installs 38, and the budget dropped to 2,000 won a day. On 1.1.9, 15 of 18 players solved R005 between October 30 and November 5. This verdict also used the cut line. In the budget rule, 7-day ROAS of 100% appears only as a condition for raising the budget, and the daily analyses' verdicts have no break-even row. The next verdict is due November 9, and its table should have that row.

FAQ

Is a low CPI on game ads a reason to spend more?

Only if it is below break-even CPI. The cheapest CPI here was $0.20 with 0.1% ROAS, and the best campaign, Block Turn KR, still paid about 30 times its break-even.

Why do Google Ads install conversions and GA4 first_open differ?

We did not find the cause; we measured the ratio. Block Turn EN had 94 Ads conversions to 24 GA4 first opens (26%), KR had 38 to 19 (50%), and the October 17 to 23 KR retest had 55 to 25 (45%).

How do you compute break-even CPI for an ad-funded game?

Break-even CPI is the expected ad revenue per installer: expected active days x ARPDAU. Expected active days are 1 for install day plus the daily return rates (D1, D2, D3 and on).
