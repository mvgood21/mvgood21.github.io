We slowed a mini game down twice before checking why it was hard: players couldn't recall the answers

Beginners in Spell Rain were stuck on remembering the correct spelling, not on the falling speed. We changed the speed twice before a player model showed us that.

By SugarMount · Game development · 10 min read · Last updated November 11, 2026

When we replayed the October 15, 2026 build (vc99, words falling in 24 seconds instead of 12) through a player model, the beginner profile lost a heart within the first minute in 100% of 300 runs. That build existed because the operator had said the on-screen keyboard covered half the sky and they "couldn't type anything." On the morning of October 17 the operator came back with "even 62 points is hard" and "I can't think of the answers." The rebuilt version (vc100) kept the model's beginner alive for 3.4 minutes and to level 3, but finished games in the first two weeks after release averaged level 2.0.

Sources: git history of the SugarSpeller repository (4867e24, 73d8ce8, 1948777, 78d7283), the October 14 to 17 working-session log, the release note, and the monitoring log (GA4, October 17 to 31). Constants were rechecked on the remote default branch on November 9, 2026.

Two speed changes in two days; after the first one, still too hard

Spell Rain is a mini game inside SugarSpeller, our Korean spelling checker. Misspelled words fall from the top, for example 되요 (the common misspelling of 돼요, "it works"), and the player types the correct form to clear them. A word that reaches the ground costs one of five hearts; every 10 correct words raise the level. The first build on October 14 (4867e24) dropped a word every 3.2 seconds with a 12-second fall, and a code comment reasoned that typing "안 돼요" on a phone takes 2 to 4 seconds, so a first-timer would clear the first ten comfortably.

At 21:39 KST on October 15 the operator, playing on a real phone, said the keyboard halved the visible sky and the words came too fast, and asked for 50% slower. The agent answered "I'll halve the fall speed" and committed 73d8ce8 48 minutes later: 24-second fall, 9-second floor, a new word every 4 seconds. Slower words bunched up and overlapped on a Galaxy S20, so the same commit also forced consecutive words at least two lanes apart.

At 07:56 on October 17 the operator wrote that every word on screen is wrong, 62 points is hard, and the answers just don't come to mind. This time the agent named the cause: the screen only shows the wrong form, so a player who doesn't know where a word like 희안하다 is wrong can't type it no matter how much time there is. Then at 08:05 the operator also asked for slower words and fewer at once, and at 08:06 the agent changed the constants first: 30-second fall, 6-second spawn, three words on screen. A moment later the operator wrote again, roughly: you're supposed to be the level-design expert, do I have to keep asking like this, do it properly.

An unknown word reaches the ground even with a 24-second fall

SpellRainBalanceTest, written at 08:08, runs the real game engine 300 times each with a beginner, an average and a skilled player model. The model looks at the lowest word first. If it knows the answer, it spends a recall time, a per-syllable typing time and a submit time; the beginner uses 2.5 s, 0.9 s per syllable and 0.8 s. Whether it knows a word is decided once per word: the beginner knows easy words with probability 0.80, medium 0.50 and hard 0.25. Words it doesn't know are left alone until a hint appears.

vc99 had no hints and no tiers, so every word could fall from level 1, and the screen showed only the wrong form: a player who doesn't already know the right one has nothing to work from. Over the 124 bundled words the model uses (hand-rated that day as 47 easy, 40 medium, 37 hard), the beginner knows about 54%. The other 46% reach the ground untyped whether the fall takes 12 seconds or 24, and each costs a heart. Slowing down only moves the moment the heart is lost, which is why this build's beginner lost a heart within the first minute in 100% of runs.

The October 15 complaint about speed was real (the keyboard did hide half the sky), but the recall problem sat underneath it the whole time.

vc100 added help with recall. Levels 1 and 2 use only easy words, levels 3 and 4 add medium ones, and level 5 opens everything. The differing letters of each wrong word are drawn red and underlined; the code comment says this tells the player where to fix the word but leaves how to them. Once a word passes the halfway line, the correct form appears faintly under it, and typing it then earns half points. Each level-up returns one heart, up to five.

The speed relief the operator asked for went in too. On October 15 the spawn interval had stopped at 4 seconds because doubling it seemed boring; this time it went to 6 seconds, and the on-screen cap went from 8 to 3 at level 1. Below are the options on record, plus the comparisons nobody ran:

The first test modeled a typist who knows every answer

The first build's test (4867e24), 처음_하는_사람도_첫_10개는_하트를_안_잃는다 ("a first-timer loses no heart in the first ten") assumes a player who looks at the lowest word and always types the correct answer 3.5 seconds later.

if (typingUntil < 0) typingUntil = e.elapsedMs + 3_500
if (e.elapsedMs >= typingUntil) {
    assertEquals(Result.HIT, e.submit(e.lowest()!!.word.right))

In that model the player knows everything, so the only difficulty knobs are fall time and spawn interval. When the operator said "too fast," those were the only numbers the agent had to turn. The October 17 model put the chance of not knowing into the same spot:

// lowest first: a word it knows, or one already showing the hint
val pick = e.drops.sortedByDescending { it.y }.firstOrNull { d ->
    knows.getOrPut(d.id) { rnd.nextDouble() < p.know(d.word.tier) } || d.hinted
} ?: continue

When players say a game is too hard, don't start by changing the speed. Write down the steps a player takes for one item (see, recall, type, submit) and put the probability of not knowing into the model first. The rule saved to the agent's project memory that day says the same: split difficulty feedback into speed, density, cognitive load and learning aids before touching numbers, and ship the change with a simulation table.

The record says "reproduced, then tuned"; the session shows the reverse order

The 1948777 commit message says the curve was tuned to targets after reproducing vc99 (beginner 86 points), and the changelog says the numbers were not changed by feel. The session log has a different order.

Oct 17, KSTActionResult
08:06Constants changed (30 s, 6 s, three on screen)No model yet
08:08Balance test written, target assertions includedBefore the first run
08:09First runBeginner 3.4 min, level 3, 675 points, 0% first-minute heart loss
08:10Same model on the vc99 engineBeginner 1.1 min, level 1, 86 points, 100%
08:14Run with the level 1–2 easy-only rule and level-up heart removedTwo engine tests fail; beginner 3.2 min
08:27Commit 1948777, internal test uploadUnit tests 531/531

The constants were set before the model existed and never changed after the first run passed. The simulator checked a hand-picked curve; it did not find one. Because the targets were written before the first run, the check still means something, but anyone reading only the commit would assume the model produced the numbers.

The release note says the replay "matches the user's 62 points." In the vc99 replay the beginner's median score was 86, 24 points above what the operator scored by hand. The average and skilled profiles scored 216 and 1,066, so the beginner was simply the closest of the three; the model still played better than the person.

The operator's "I can't think of the answers" at 07:56 named the bottleneck first. Right after the 08:06 constant change the operator asked whether they had to keep asking like this, and instead of shipping another build for the operator to try, the agent wrote the balance test at 08:08.

Real players averaged level 2.0, below the model's 3

Version 1.6.030 began a staged production rollout on October 17, and the November 1 review (78d7283) recorded GA4 numbers for October 17 to 31. 133 users opened the game (267 times), 87 started it (207 games), and 35 users finished 93 games, 45% of the games started. Finished games averaged level 189/93 = 2.0 and 127 seconds (2.1 minutes). The review marked the design target (beginner median of at least 3 minutes and level 3) as missed.

The two numbers are not directly comparable. The model value is a median over 300 beginner runs; the field value is a mean over all players, beginners and experts mixed. level was registered in GA4 only as a metric, so no median was available, and rain_over counts only finished games.

The knowledge probabilities (0.80, 0.50, 0.25 for the beginner) and the word tiers were set by hand, and the only real score used to calibrate them was one person's 62 points. The model also has no screen: it knows nothing about the keyboard covering half the sky, which is where the October 15 complaint started.

We copied the 73d8ce8 engine to replay the 62-point build

  1. Write down the steps for one item and give each a time and a probability of not knowing. This game used recall, per-syllable typing, submit and hint-reading times, plus a knowledge probability per tier.
  2. Take the engine from the build the person actually played and run the same model on it. Here the 73d8ce8 engine file was copied into the test folder under a new class name. If the result is far from their score, fix the model before the constants.
  3. Write the targets as test assertions before the first run: here, at most 10% of beginner runs losing a heart in the first minute, a median of 3 minutes and level 3, average players at level 5 to 8, skilled players finishing within 25 minutes.
  4. Pick constants after running the model. If you picked them first, as happened here, say so in the commit message.
  5. Remove each device in turn and confirm the balance test fails, not just an engine test.
  6. After release, log the same measures as events and register them so a median is available.

The method of choosing difficulty numbers with a player model, with a full table of candidate values, is in Puzzle game time limits: 3-star par by simulation.

Still open: a 36-second easy stretch and a refit model

The November 1 review set the next task: widen the easy stretch, for example by lengthening the level 1 and 2 fall from 30 to 36 seconds, and recalibrate SpellRainBalanceTest. On November 9 the remote default branch still has 30 seconds, 6 seconds and three words. The review's rule says that if the real median level is below 2, the simulator model gets refit to field data before the easy stretch grows, but that median can't be read yet because of how level was registered. The events also don't record hint use or missed words by tier, so a refit still couldn't correct the knowledge probabilities from field data.

FAQ

Can game difficulty balance be checked with test code?

If the engine is plain logic with no Android dependency, a JUnit test can play hundreds of games through a player model. This game runs 300 games each for beginner, average and skilled profiles and asserts on median survival time and level.

If players say a typing game is too hard, should I slow it down?

Not if some items are ones players don't know: those reach the ground at any speed. Our earlier build lost a heart in the first minute in 100% of beginner-model runs even with a 24-second fall; the rebuild combined slower pacing with highlighted wrong letters, a halfway hint and easy words first.

What do you calibrate a player model against?

The build the complaining player actually played, and their score on it. We ran the same model on the vc99 engine the operator had scored 62 on, and it scored 86.
