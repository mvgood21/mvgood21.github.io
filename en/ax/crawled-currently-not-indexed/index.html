Crawled - currently not indexed: a core update log

Crawled - currently not indexed is not a block. Google fetched the page and decided not to keep it. After the November 2026 core update, this site went from 241 indexed pages to 54 in three days.

By Stephen · Website and search · 12 min read · Last updated November 28, 2026

On November 21, 2026, a URL Inspection run over every URL in this site's sitemap found 241 indexed pages. Three days later there were 54. Almost everything that left read "Crawled - currently not indexed" in Search Console, and none of those pages was blocked by robots.txt, tagged noindex or failing on the server. This was inside the window of Google's November core update, which ran from November 10 to 24.

This is a running record: what the status means, how we told a technical problem from a quality judgment, what we changed and what we are deliberately not doing. Recovery is not proven. We measure again on December 6 and December 13 and will add the results here.

241 indexed pages became 54 in three days

Every night at 03:00 Korea time, this site inspects every sitemap URL through the URL Inspection API (a "full run" below). On the morning of November 22 that record showed weekly impressions on the new domain property down to 38 from 490 the week before, a 92% drop, and 42 pages as "Crawled". A day later the indexed count went from 219 to 119, and the day after that to 54. On the 24th the site owner chose to change the shape of the site instead of patching pages one at a time (see "What we changed" below).

The 14 on November 28 is out of 178 URLs, after we set 132 note pages to noindex and took them out of the sitemap. Counting only the 162 URLs that stayed in the sitemap the whole time, it was 135 on November 20, 38 on November 24 and 14 on November 28.

Impressions fell before the index did. The site moved from a github.io address to its own domain on October 31 and still 301-redirects the old host, so there are two Search Console properties to read: one for the old address, one for the new domain.

The new property broke on November 12, two days after the rollout began. The old one held for another week and then dropped to almost nothing on November 20. Both went down, so the domain move alone does not explain it.

What the status means

Google's Page indexing report help defines it as a page that "was crawled by Google but not indexed", and says that because Google doesn't index every page on the web, this is a common message that doesn't necessarily need action. The same page tells you not to expect every URL on your site to be indexed.

On November 10, about three hours after the update was announced, someone asked on Bluesky whether a site with many pages in this state should noindex them, redirect them or wait. John Mueller of Google replied: "Usually this is a sign that Google's quality systems are unconvinced about your site (or convinced in the way that you don't want)." In the same post he added that just making the site smaller doesn't significantly raise its overall quality. That second sentence collides with one of our own decisions, covered under "What we are not doing".

The most useful fact in our data was the crawl date. Of the 180 pages that were indexed on November 20 and "Crawled" on November 24, 142 still had the same last-crawl date as on the 20th. Google did not fetch them again; it changed its decision about pages it already had. Nothing that changed on those pages triggered the drop, which also means that editing them does nothing until Google crawls them again. Google's Search Status Dashboard showed no crawling or indexing incident in that period; the only ranking entries were the November core update and, on November 24, the pre-announcement of a December update.

The fields to read in URL Inspection

Below are the fields that matter from two API responses on midday November 28, copied as returned. In A every field passes and the status is still "Crawled". In B the canonical Google picked is still the old host. Neither page is indexed, but the work is different.

  • Last crawl: if it is earlier than your edit, Google has not seen the edit. Before judging a fix, check that this date has moved past the day you made it.
  • User-declared vs Google-selected canonical: if they match, canonicalization is not your problem. If they differ, as in B, that page has a duplicate or migration issue, not a quality verdict. The help page notes this value can be a few hours out of date.
  • Sitemap last read: the sitemap report's "Last read" date (lastDownloaded in the API). Our four sitemaps were last read on November 19 (Korea time) and have not been read since, nine days now. Pages crawled again per day went from 9-25 on November 14-24 to 1, 8, 2 and 1 on November 25-28. The run shrank to 178 URLs, but as a share it still fell from 3-11% to 0.6-4.5%.

To get the same fields from the API, send this as an owner of the property. In the Search Console UI they sit under "Page indexing" in the URL Inspection result.

POST https://searchconsole.googleapis.com/v1/urlInspection/index:inspect
{"inspectionUrl": "https://example.com/page/", "siteUrl": "sc-domain:example.com", "languageCode": "en-US"}

The domain move was only part of the story

Our first suspect was the October 31 domain move. Google's site move guide warns about ranking fluctuations while it recrawls, and pages like B are real: five are left, last crawled between October 31 and November 9, so Google has not finished applying the 301s. But if the move were the cause, impressions would shift from the old property to the new one. Instead the new property dropped first and the old one followed. The move alone can't explain the 151.

The second suspect was article length. On November 20 the five Korean notes in "Crawled" had a median body of 5,912 characters against 5,690 for the other 91, so short pages were not the ones leaving.

What we got wrong

Between October 18 and November 23, 37 days, this site published 101 AX notes (write-ups of what happened while building our apps), each in Korean and English. Through November 8 that was three a day on every publishing day, all in the same mould: a cover and two text-card figures. On November 13 AdSense rejected the site for "replicated content" and "low value content", a similar complaint from another system (AdSense rejected for low value, replicated content).

Pages and search demand had pulled apart.

Notes were two thirds of the pages, while two thirds of the impressions went to app guides such as resizing an ID photo or converting PNG to JPG. Web clicks on the new property for those eleven days came to 9 (15 on the old property).

The second mistake came from the agent side. On November 26-28, while crawling had almost stopped, the scheduled agent rewrote titles and descriptions of pages that had a few impressions, seven commits in all. The five that landed before the November 28 03:00 run all touched pages last crawled between November 3 and 21, and apart from one page recrawled after that day's indexing request, Google never saw the edits.

What we are not doing, and why

If most of a site turns "Crawled" at once, look at the crawl dates before you touch a single page.

Not doingWhy
Requesting indexing for every dropped pageA request can bring the crawl forward; it does not change the decision. Google's docs say requesting the same URL repeatedly won't speed it up, and there is a daily quota. In October, when our pages were merely undiscovered, 5 of 7 requested pages were indexed within four hours (linked note below). On November 28 we requested 4 pages; they were crawled again at 12:26-12:32 and were still "Crawled" at the 12:40 check
Expecting a smaller site alone to win the index backGoogle reserves deletion for content that can't be salvaged, and Mueller says shrinking the site alone doesn't significantly raise its quality
Daily title and description tweaksGoogle cannot see them until it recrawls, which is exactly what happened to our seven edits. On a site where crawling has slowed you can't even tell when an edit will be read
Publishing more of the same template while waitingIt adds more of what may have caused the verdict. Apart from this record and the AdSense rejection record, this site publishes no new notes until December 6

We did noindex 66 notes on November 24 ourselves. Taking Mueller at his word, that won't bring recovery on its own. Only 21 of those 66 had any web impressions in November 1-11, 12 at most for one note. We count the change as tidying, not as a recovery lever. The 35 notes we kept fell too, from 54 of 70 URLs indexed on November 20 to 8 on November 28, and the app pages went from 41 of 52 to 3 of 68. Pages with real demand dropped along with the rest, so the verdict is about the whole site.

What we changed: fewer, deeper pages that do the job

Google's core update guidance points sites that dropped to a set of self-assessment questions (listed in the "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" doc). Does the page offer original information or analysis? Would a reader feel they need to search again? Are you using extensive automation to produce content on many topics? Google's spam policies define "scaled content abuse" as generating many pages for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings, with little value to users. On the automation question, our honest answer was yes.

So we changed how many pages there are and what each page does.

  • November 24: of 101 notes, only the 35 that Google had kept indexed or that had received search queries stay indexable, and the AX hub now opens with notes grouped by problem.
  • November 24-25: the four photo guide pages that people actually reached from search got a tool that runs in the browser. Resize an ID photo for Korean application uploads had the most web impressions on the site in November 1-11 (146 for the Korean page), and now the page itself shrinks a photo under 100 KB or 200 KB without uploading it. PNG to JPG, the 100 KB fitter and the selfie flip work the same way.
  • November 28: a fifth tool, HEIC to JPG, went live, and the 13 series pages (26 in Korean and English) were set to noindex. We also requested indexing for the four Korean tool pages. They were crawled again between 12:26 and 12:32 Korea time and were still "Crawled" at 12:40.

Google's guidance says it can take several months for its systems to recognize a change, and that if nothing moves after a few months you may be waiting for the next core update. Even if these four pages get indexed, that won't mean the site-wide verdict has turned.

What we measure on December 6 and 13

The bar was set on November 24 and stays: at least 70% of sitemap URLs indexed, and at least 200 weekly impressions on the new property. On November 28 we had 14 of 178 (7.9%) and 14 impressions for November 21-27.

  1. December 6, 03:00: inspect every sitemap URL and compare last-crawl dates with the November 28 list. Count separately how many recrawled pages came back indexed.

  2. Same day: check whether the sitemap "Last read" date has moved off November 19, and whether the four tool pages changed state.

  3. December 13: run it again and compare weekly impressions. Whatever the result, it goes into this note as a "December 13 update" section.

One thing could blur the reading. On November 24 Google pre-announced a December 2026 core update on the Search Status Dashboard, scheduled for December. If it starts before December 13, we can't separate our changes from the update, and we will say so. This is one small site of 178 URLs, observed without a control group.

FAQ

Is "Crawled - currently not indexed" an error?

No. Google fetched the page and chose not to index it, and its help page says the status doesn't necessarily need action. But if most of a site lands there within days while every other URL Inspection field passes, as it did here, a site-wide quality judgment is the likely reason.

Will requesting indexing fix it?

For pages Google hasn't crawled yet ("Discovered"), a request speeds things up. For pages it already crawled and declined, a request may only get them crawled again with the same result. That is what happened to the four pages we requested on November 28.

How long until indexing comes back after a core update?

Google says anywhere from a few days to several months, possibly not until the next core update, and recommends waiting at least a full week after an update completes before comparing data. Our own measurements go into this note on December 6 and 13.
