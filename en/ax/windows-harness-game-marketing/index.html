Game app marketing automation: 17 days of GA4 analysis, Google Ads budgets and TikTok and YouTube Shorts run by an AI harness

Marketing for our two puzzle games is split into six scheduled jobs. Anything that spends money got a hard cap and an approval inbox before the AI was allowed near it.

By SugarMount · Play Store and monetization · 11 min read · Last updated November 11, 2026

Since October 25, 2026, a harness on a Windows PC has run the marketing for Arrow Knots and Block Turn. Every day Claude Code reads the GA4 and ad numbers, moves Google Ads daily budgets inside fixed limits, and makes a short video that goes out to TikTok, Instagram and YouTube. Along the way it found two campaigns that had quietly stopped on an end date, and it logged the same video at 1,279 views on YouTube and 1 on TikTok (as of November 6).

The six jobs behind the game marketing

Collecting numbers, judging them, posting, carrying out approved changes and watching for failures are separate jobs. The day after the harness hit its weekly usage limit on October 30, posting moved to the middle-tier model, Sonnet, to save quota; the daily analysis and the approval runner stay on the larger Opus. Claude works Google Ads, TikTok and YouTube through the Chrome extension, in a Chrome profile already signed in to those accounts (jobs that need it start claude with --chrome), and it reads the page before and after every change. In the dedicated config folder the Chrome extension connects only with --chrome, and there were no Gmail or Slack connectors (checked Oct 25), so alerts go to an alert row in the Notion report, a Windows notification and the log.

This is how the three app daily jobs did from October 25 to November 10.

Seven of the 51 cells failed, for two reasons. On October 30 the weekly usage limit stopped all three daily jobs and all three game social runs that day (12:07, 16:07 and 20:07). On November 4 the Korean app's job tripped over changes an earlier run had left in its worktree, and from the morning of November 5 until 09:38 on the 6th, uncommitted edits to the harness code failed the prepare-step check for all three apps (part 4).

GA4 numbers carry a source tag, and small ones a fraction

The game daily job checks each headline number from the snapshot against one more source: the GA4 API, the AdMob API or Play. Each of those numbers gets a tag for where it came from. When two sources disagree, both values go in with the reason; when the reason is unknown, the report says so.

New users <n> [snapshot] / <n> [GA4]     two sources disagree: both, plus a reason
R001 solve rate 6/8 (75%) [GA4]          under 20 people: show the fraction
Budget <campaign>: before → after [Ads]  the value re-read on screen after saving

The GA4 helper script, tools/ga4_report.py, drops test phones and Play pre-launch devices by default. Why it does is in GA4 said 19 installs yesterday and 8 were real.

The solve rate of Block Turn's first puzzle, R001, is the share of players who start it and clear it; the baseline on version 1.1.6 is 6 of 8, or 75%. One more player clearing it would make it 88%. So below 20 people the harness writes the fraction next to the percentage, and a gap that one person could flip is called "the same".

Google Ads budgets: the caps came first

On October 30 the operator handed daily budget changes to the harness, and wrote down the ceiling, the floor and the pace before anything else. ROAS here is ad revenue from the users a campaign brought in, divided by what the campaign spent. Amounts are in Korean won.

RuleDetail
Caps₩5,000 a day per app, ₩12,000 across all live campaigns. Approval cannot lift them
Raise7-day ROAS at least 100% and at least 20 installs in 7 days: +₩500 to ₩1,000
Cut7-day ROAS under 30% after at least 7 days running: -₩500 to ₩1,000, floor ₩1,000
PaceEach campaign changes at most once in 3 days, by ₩1,000 at most, so its learning phase doesn't reset
ReadRead every live campaign's budget on screen before changing, check the caps, re-read after saving. If the screen can't be read, change nothing
To the inboxTurning off, new campaigns, stopping, bids, targeting, swapping ad creatives

Both campaigns stopped on end dates left in their settings, and the harness missed one of them at first. The November 2 run found Block Turn's end date and sent it to the inbox, but its record says nothing about the end date on Arrow Knots KR, which fell on that same day and which the run had just cut. The November 4 run saw zero spend and zero installs for the day before, opened the settings page and found it.

During the November 5 cut, the first save click missed because the panel changed height, and reopening it expanded the bidding section by mistake. The harness cancelled without touching bids, then re-read ₩2,000 in the refreshed campaign list to confirm it.

On November 8 Arrow Knots KR's 7-day ROAS jumped, and the budget stayed put. It was still below the raise rule, and interstitial (full-screen) ad clicks were abnormally high again on November 7 after November 1 and 5 (8 clicks on 3 impressions that day), so the report flagged that ROAS might be inflated. The next day's record, with no click outlier, showed the 7-day ROAS falling back and noted that this fit the November 8 suspicion. Spend and installs per campaign are covered in Four Google Ads app campaigns and break-even CPI (cost per install).

How one video gets to TikTok, Instagram and YouTube

Since October 31 the harness has made and posted a video every day. From November 8, on the operator's instruction, each app group (the Korean app, the utilities, the games) gets one video a day, and the two games split the week for theirs: Arrow Knots on Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday, Block Turn on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday.

The Q2 quality gate came after the operator looked at the videos on November 1 and called the quality far too low. A closer look found that the game videos had no sound, all three app groups' videos were slideshows of still screens, and the small text sat at the edges where TikTok's own buttons covered it. Now a video must pass a machine check (q2_check.py) and a score from a separate Claude that didn't make it before it enters the queue, where its row carries a mark such as [Q2 9/10 F2]: the score, then a format number.

Apart from q4 at 1 view, the ten TikTok posts sat between 83 and 135 views, while four of the five YouTube posts beat the 190 baseline. In October, TikTok videos posted 2 to 4 minutes apart got zero views, so TikTok posts are now at least two hours apart.

On November 9 TikTok's content check ran past four minutes, the game social run stopped altogether, and none of the three channels got a post. Since November 11 each channel is handled on its own: a failed channel is logged and the others still go out, and the TikTok check gets up to ten minutes.

Whether views turn into installs is measured through the Play link in the YouTube description. The tracking part is utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=shorts&utm_campaign=<queue id>; inside a Play link it goes after referrer= with = written as %3D and & as %26. Installs from that link then show up in GA4's first-user source (firstUserSourceMedium) as youtube / shorts. On November 8 the game social job guessed the app's package ID for that link and had to fix it; the rule since then is to take the value from the config file and nowhere else.

Three game requests that went through the approval inbox

The approval inbox is a Notion table. A daily job adds a row with a request ID (for games, GAM- plus month, day and a counter) and its numbers, set to Waiting. When the operator flips it to Approved, the dispatcher calls the approval runner. Each daily job reads the last 14 days of rows first, so a rejected idea isn't raised again. Which actions go straight through and which wait for a person is set out in the permission table for an AI marketer that runs four times a day.

Three days in shadow mode, then live

From October 25 to 27 the harness ran in shadow mode: the same analysis and reports, but commits, releases, posts and inbox requests were only written down as "would have done". It succeeded for all three apps on all three days, but the old schedules had been stopped, so only the game app had a same-day comparison. How a comparison report misjudged those old schedules is told in part 1. Going live on October 27 meant turning the old schedule off (run both and the same day gets two releases and two posts), noting any release in progress, committing the mode change to live, registering the jobs again and opening the next day's results.

If you're starting out: reports first, budgets last

  1. Run one job in shadow mode for about three days and compare it with the analysis you used to do by hand. Make it tag every number with its source and write fractions below 20 people.
  2. Switch it to live for records and reports only.
  3. Build the quality gate before any posting: check that the video has sound, isn't a slideshow of still screens, and keeps its text away from the edges.
  4. Budgets come last. Write down the caps, the raise and cut thresholds, the three-day rule and read-before-and-after, plus an end-date check, which even this harness doesn't have yet.

Don't hand a budget to an AI until the caps are written down as numbers.

The jobs only run while the PC is on and you're signed in (part 4), and every job draws on one subscription's weekly usage limit, so they can all stop together as they did on October 30. As of November 11 the end-date check appears nowhere in the shared rules or the game instructions, and the November 4 record notes that the Korean app's campaign end date wasn't checked. Views went up, but installs through the Shorts links are still zero: the November 9 and 10 records show a 7-day youtube / shorts total of 0 for both games, though the links only went in on November 8, so most of that window predates them.

Still open: the Nov 12 budget review and Nov 13 creative check

Block Turn KR still meets the cut rule at the ₩1,000 floor. The November 12 review, the first to include the latest cut, decides in the inbox whether to turn it off. The effect of the video creatives added under GAM-1106-1 is due to be judged on November 13, so it isn't measured yet, and installs from the Shorts links are still being counted.
