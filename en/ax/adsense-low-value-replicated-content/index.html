AdSense rejected for low value, replicated content

An AdSense "low value content" rejection judges the whole site. On ours the problem was not copied sentences but 101 notes that few people searched for, plus thin list and policy pages.

By Stephen · Website and search · 13 min read · Last updated November 28, 2026

On November 13, AdSense sent this site back with the status "Needs attention" and two reasons: low value content, and Google-served ads on screens with replicated content. We had never copied anyone's article, so at first the second reason made no sense. When we pulled up the site as it stood that evening and measured it, 71 of the 84 notes had under 5% of their five-word sequences in common with any other note. We had also published 101 development notes in one template over five weeks, and only 8 of them reached 10 search impressions between October 31 and November 25.

This is an in-progress record. As of November 28 we have not asked for another review and the site is not approved. The conditions we set before pressing the review button, and the date we check them again (December 6), are at the end.

The two policy lines behind the rejection

Both reasons come from the "inventory value" part of the Google Publisher Policies. One covers screens without publisher content or with low value content. The other covers replicated content: content copied or embedded from elsewhere "without adding any value by curation or providing commentary". Its list of examples includes rewritten or lightly modified copies, and also "automatically generated content without manual review or curation".

Replication is not only about other sites. AdSense's content and user experience guide asks you to check that fragments are not copied from elsewhere and that the same content is not duplicated within a page or across multiple pages of your own site. The same guide asks whether your content offers substantial value and originality compared with other sites on the same subjects. Google publishes no thresholds (no post count, word count or percentage). The "what the reviewer looks at" column below is our reading of these documents and our own record, not a review procedure Google has described.

Our record: 101 notes in five weeks

The site has pages for our Android apps and a set of development notes (AX notes) about building those apps with an AI coding agent. Notes went out almost daily from October 18, and from late October most were written by an AI agent running as a scheduled job. On every publishing day up to November 8 the count was exactly three (git history). AdSense could not go ahead on our github.io address, so on October 31 we moved to sugarmount21.com and applied there.

The rejection and the search drop arrived in the same week. Daily web impressions were 54 to 102 from November 1 to 11 and then 1 to 9 from November 13. URLs Google kept indexed went from 235 of 284 on November 20 to 54 of 300 on November 24 and 14 of 178 (7.9%) on November 28. The denominator fell from 300 to 178 because the November 24 noindex took pages out of the sitemap; the index had already dropped from 219 to 119 on November 22 to 23, before that change. AdSense review and search ranking are separate systems, so we cannot prove they made one judgment. We can only say both moved the same way in the same week.

No copied text, and few searching

Our first guess was boilerplate. Every page carries the same menu, footer, inquiry box and a one-line notice that an AI agent drafted the note, and we thought that might read as replication. So we checked out the site as of the evening of the rejection (commit 91d1ad1), split each page's visible text into blocks, and measured how much of each page appears word for word on three or more of the 159 Korean pages.

The repeated part of a note was mostly menu, footer and related-note lists. Across all 84 notes, 13 overlapped with another note by more than 5% of five-word sequences, and 11 of those were parts of one series (highest 9.7%). The real duplicates were the privacy policies: the three image apps' policies differed by little more than the app name, and over 90% of their text matched other policies.

Our second guess, that the notes were too short, was wrong too. The median unique text of the 84 notes was 7,145 characters and none was under 1,500. Thin pages sat outside the notes: 38 of the 159 Korean pages had under 1,500 characters of their own or were more than half repeated text. None was a note. Thirteen were series index pages (median unique text 313 characters), nine were topic or paging lists, six were privacy policies, six were app pages and four were other pages.

Summing Korean and English pages per note, the Search Console API showed a different problem for October 31 to November 25.

All 101 notes together got 289 impressions while the app pages got 624. People reached the site with photo problems such as shrinking an ID photo or converting PNG to JPG on a Galaxy phone, while most of the site was a log of building apps with an agent. Trace it back and the scheduled job picked topics to fill three slots a day without checking whether anyone searched for them, and every note had the same shape: one cover and two text-card figures. The agent filled the slots it was given; no step in the publishing procedure checked search demand until November 24.

Publishing more did not help

A common piece of advice after this rejection is to write a few more good posts and reapply. On one Korean community board, a person rejected for the same reason was told to add three to five posts and try again; another blogger reports approval with fewer than 20 posts, all programming tutorials. Both are personal accounts, not rules, but the second fits our record better: focus seems to count for more than volume.

We slowed down but kept publishing, 17 more notes between November 14 and 23, and neither indexing nor impressions came back. The core update overlaps that window, so we cannot isolate what the new notes did. Google's helpful content self-assessment asks whether you are "producing lots of content on many different topics in hopes that some of it might perform well in search results" and whether you use extensive automation, and the spam policies say scaled content abuse applies no matter how the content is created. Our answer to both was yes. We now publish nothing without a confirmed search query.

We dropped two other options. Deleting notes: Google's core update guidance calls deletion a last resort for content that cannot be salvaged. Mass-noindexing every note with zero impressions, planned on November 22: 27 of the 30 notes at zero then were published after November 10, so we could not tell missing demand from missing data.

What we changed on November 24 to 28

We re-chose what to keep indexed by demand. The 35 notes that Google kept indexed or that real queries reached stay; the other 66 are noindex but not deleted, and every link to them still works. The AX hub now opens with five groups of problems we hit while building the apps, instead of a feed of the latest posts.

The ad code follows the same rule. With mode set to "review" in the site's data/ads.json, the build puts only the AdSense loader tag (the script that loads ads) in the head of notes that are indexable and long enough (1,500 characters of Korean text, or 600 English words), and no ad units. List pages, app pages and policies get nothing. Pages with the loader went from 201 to 70 (the 35 notes in two languages).

The pages people actually search got something to use. On November 24 and 25 we added in-browser tools, which process the file without uploading it, to the guides for ID photo size, PNG to JPG, photos under 100 KB and un-mirroring selfies, and on November 28 a HEIC to JPG tool made it five. The most-seen of these, the ID photo size guide, had 151 impressions on its Korean page since October 31. Publishing is paused until December 6 and capped at two notes a week after that.

On November 28 the 13 series index pages went noindex as well; 11 of them had under 1,500 characters of their own.

How to measure your own site

Write these four numbers down before you request review, so you can compare before and after. Copies from other sites are a separate check: search a few distinctive sentences in quotes, as in the table.

  1. Index share: in Search Console open Indexing > Pages, pick "All submitted pages" above the chart, and divide Indexed by the number of sitemap URLs. Our figure (14 of 178 on November 28) comes from inspecting each sitemap URL through the URL Inspection API, so it can differ from the report by a day or two. "Crawled - currently not indexed" means Google read a page and chose not to keep it. The Page indexing report help calls it common, but when most of a site is in that state we read it as pointing the same way as AdSense. How we check those pages one by one is in Crawled - currently not indexed: a core update log.

  2. Pages with impressions: in Performance > Search results, set 28 days and open the Pages tab. Count rows with at least one impression and compare with your sitemap. We had 121 pages with impressions on October 31 to November 11 and 45 on November 12 to 25.

  3. Repeated share and thin pages: save the script below as repeat.py and run python repeat.py <build folder> (standard library only). The first column is the share of text that appears word for word on 3 or more pages, the second is text unique to that page. Count pages above 50% repeated or under 1,500 unique characters (1,500 is our cutoff, not a Google number). Our table above counts Korean pages and body text only, so this script's numbers differ slightly.

    import sys, glob, collections
from html.parser import HTMLParser
class Blocks(HTMLParser):
    def __init__(s): super().__init__(); s.out, s.buf, s.skip = [], [], 0
    def cut(s):
        t = " ".join("".join(s.buf).split()); s.buf = []
        if t: s.out.append(t)
    def handle_starttag(s, tag, a): s.skip += tag in ("script", "style"); s.cut()
    def handle_endtag(s, tag): s.skip -= tag in ("script", "style"); s.cut()
    def handle_data(s, d):
        if not s.skip: s.buf.append(d)
pages = {}
for f in glob.glob(sys.argv[1] + "/**/*.html", recursive=True):
    p = Blocks(); p.feed(open(f, encoding="utf-8").read()); p.cut(); pages[f] = p.out
seen = collections.Counter(b for bl in pages.values() for b in set(bl))
for f, bl in sorted(pages.items()):
    total = sum(map(len, bl)) or 1
    rep = sum(len(b) for b in bl if seen[b] >= 3)
    print(f"{rep / total:4.0%} repeated {total - rep:6} own  {f}")

  4. Publishing pace: on a static site, count the dates new posts arrived with something like git log --diff-filter=A --format=%ad --date=short -- "posts/*" | sort | uniq -c; on a CMS, sort the post list by date. The same count every day, or dozens of posts within a few weeks, puts you in the last row of the table.

Why we have not pressed review yet

We set four conditions: at least 70% of sitemap URLs indexed, at least 200 web impressions a week on the new domain, tools on all five image pages with proven demand, and the 13 series pages sorted out. The first two are our own thresholds, set below the pre-rejection level (69 to 83% indexed on November 11 to 20, 486 impressions on November 1 to 7). The tools and the series pages were done on November 28. The other two stood at 7.9% indexed (November 28) and 22 impressions (November 19 to 25). We will not press the button until all four are met, and we measure again on December 6.

There are two reasons to wait. The only outside numbers that can show whether our changes worked are indexing and impressions, and both are still worse than at the rejection. And AdSense's site status help says a review usually takes a few days but can take 2 to 4 weeks, and that removing and re-adding the site can delay it; Google's core update guidance adds that it can take several months for its systems to recognize improvements. Requesting review right after the changes would show a site that is still changing.

Waiting makes less sense when the reason has nothing to do with content (account or ownership verification, for example), or when the four measurements above turn up only a handful of problem pages and you have already fixed them.

What we would do differently, and what is left

Starting over, we would pick our own domain before applying (eight days passed between the github.io setup on October 23 and the new request on October 31), write only topics with confirmed searches instead of aiming at a post count, and noindex policy and list pages from the day they were made. We should also have put the tools on the app pages before writing more notes.

What is left is the December 6 measurement. Once the conditions hold we request review, and whatever the answer, approval or another rejection, it goes into this note as a dated addition.

FAQ

Does AdSense "replicated content" only mean copying other sites?

No. The AdSense guide also asks you to check for the same content repeated across pages of your own site, and the policy examples include automatically generated content without manual review. On our site the real overlap was in privacy policies that differed only by app name.

How many more posts do I need for approval?

Google publishes no post count. Before adding posts, count how many of your existing posts get any search impressions.

If a page is noindex, does the reviewer skip it?

Unknown. Noindex only keeps a page out of the search index, and Google has not said whether AdSense reviewers look at such pages. That is why we also removed the ad code from every page we noindexed.
